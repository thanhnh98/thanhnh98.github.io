About Hanukkah

Eight nights of candles, starting at sundown.

Also known as: Chanukah, Hanukah, Festival of Lights

Hanukkah (Chanukah) is the Jewish Festival of Lights. It begins at sundown on the 25th of Kislev in the Hebrew calendar and lasts eight nights, so it usually falls between late November and late December.

The festival commemorates the rededication of the Second Temple in Jerusalem. Each night one more candle is lit on the menorah, and families enjoy foods fried in oil such as latkes and sufganiyot.

Origins & communities

Historical roots
Hanukkah commemorates the Maccabean revolt and the rededication of the Second Temple in Jerusalem in the second century BCE.
Communities & traditions
It is a Jewish festival observed by Jewish communities in Israel and throughout the diaspora, with customs that vary among families and regions.

Traditions

  • Lighting the menorah each night
  • Latkes and sufganiyot (jelly doughnuts)
  • Playing dreidel
  • Small gifts and gelt

Hanukkah Around the World

These examples cover major regional and diaspora traditions. They are representative, not an exhaustive list of every country or community that observes Hanukkah; customs can also vary within a country.

Israel

Schools close for the Hanukkah break, and bakeries fill up with sufganiyot, jelly doughnuts fried in oil.

United States & Canada

Jewish communities hold home and public hanukkiah lightings, eat latkes and sufganiyot, play dreidel and organize community events.

United Kingdom & France

Large Jewish communities mark the eight nights at home, in synagogues and at public lightings, including central-city ceremonies.

Germany and Central & Eastern Europe

Jewish communities light hanukkiot, sing and share oil-fried foods; contemporary observance also reflects the region's long and diverse Jewish histories.

Argentina, Australia & South Africa

Jewish communities across the Southern Hemisphere observe the same eight nights through family candle-lighting, synagogue and community celebrations.

How long does Hanukkah last?

Eight nights and eight days. The first candle is lit on the evening Hanukkah begins, and one more is added each night.

Why does Hanukkah start in the evening?

Days in the Hebrew calendar begin at sundown, so the first night of Hanukkah starts at sunset on the eve of 25 Kislev.
