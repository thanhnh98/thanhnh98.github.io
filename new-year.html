About New Year

The countdown to midnight on January 1.

Also known as: New Year's Day, New Year's Eve, NYE, Hogmanay

New Year's Day, January 1, is the first day of the Gregorian calendar and a public holiday in most countries. New Year's Eve parties build up to the final countdown at midnight.

Time zones mean the new year rolls around the globe over about 26 hours, starting in Kiribati and ending in American Samoa. This countdown uses your local time by default.

Origins & communities

Historical roots
January 1 was made the start of the year in the Roman calendar and later carried into the Gregorian calendar now used internationally.
Communities & traditions
It is a civil celebration across most countries, while many cultures also maintain religious, lunar or seasonal New Year traditions on other dates.

Traditions

  • Midnight fireworks
  • The Times Square ball drop in New York
  • New Year's resolutions
  • Singing "Auld Lang Syne"

New Year Around the World

These examples cover major regional and diaspora traditions. They are representative, not an exhaustive list of every country or community that observes New Year; customs can also vary within a country.

United States

Crowds count down to the ball drop in Times Square, New York. January 1 is a federal holiday.

United Kingdom & Ireland

London welcomes midnight with Big Ben and fireworks; Scotland's Hogmanay has its own gatherings, first-footing and song traditions.

Australia

Sydney Harbour's fireworks are among the first major celebrations to welcome the new year each year.

Japan

Shōgatsu is the most important holiday of the year, with toshikoshi soba on December 31 and shrine visits on New Year's Day.

Philippines

Families gather for Media Noche, the midnight meal, often with twelve round fruits for luck.

Spain & parts of Latin America

Many people eat twelve grapes with the twelve midnight chimes, one for luck in each month of the coming year.

Brazil

Large gatherings wear white, watch fireworks and, especially on the coast, make offerings or jump seven waves for good fortune.

China, Vietnam & South Korea

January 1 is observed as the civil New Year, while Spring Festival, Tết and Seollal remain separate and culturally more important lunar New Year holidays.

Middle East, Africa & South Asia

January 1 countdowns and public events are common in many cities, alongside Islamic, Ethiopian, Persian, Hindu and other New Year calendars observed on different dates.
