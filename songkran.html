About Songkran

Thai New Year and the world's biggest water fight.

Also known as: Thai New Year, Songkran Festival

Songkran is the traditional Thai New Year, celebrated as a national holiday from April 13 to 15. The name comes from a Sanskrit word for the sun's passage into Aries.

Traditionally people pour scented water over Buddha images and the hands of elders to show respect and wash away bad luck. In cities like Bangkok and Chiang Mai it has grown into a huge street water festival.

Traditions

  • Street water fights
  • Rod nam dam hua: pouring water on elders' hands
  • Making merit at temples
  • Family reunions in home provinces

Songkran Around the World

Thailand

A national holiday from April 13 to 15. Water fights fill Bangkok's Silom Road and Khao San Road and the old city of Chiang Mai.

Laos

Pi Mai Lao is celebrated with processions and water blessings, especially in Luang Prabang.

Myanmar

Thingyan, the water festival, marks the Burmese New Year in mid-April.

Cambodia

Choul Chnam Thmey, the Khmer New Year, is celebrated over three days in mid-April.

Sri Lanka

The Sinhala and Tamil New Year is celebrated on April 13–14 with family rituals at auspicious times.

How long is Songkran?

The official public holiday runs from April 13 to April 15, and celebrations in some areas last longer.

Is Songkran celebrated outside Thailand?

Yes. Similar new-year water festivals are held in Laos (Pi Mai), Myanmar (Thingyan), Cambodia and parts of southern China.
