About Songkran

Thai New Year and the world's biggest water fight.

Also known as: Thai New Year, Songkran Festival

Songkran is the traditional Thai New Year, celebrated as a national holiday from April 13 to 15. The name comes from a Sanskrit word for the sun's passage into Aries.

Traditionally people pour scented water over Buddha images and the hands of elders to show respect and wash away bad luck. In cities like Bangkok and Chiang Mai it has grown into a huge street water festival.

Origins & communities

Historical roots
Songkran takes its name from the Sanskrit sankranti, meaning an astrological transition, and developed from South and Southeast Asian solar New Year and Buddhist traditions.
Communities & traditions
It is Thailand's traditional New Year and is closely related to April New Year festivals among Lao, Khmer, Myanmar and other Tai and Southeast Asian communities.

Traditions

  • Street water fights
  • Rod nam dam hua: pouring water on elders' hands
  • Making merit at temples
  • Family reunions in home provinces

Songkran Around the World

These examples cover major regional and diaspora traditions. They are representative, not an exhaustive list of every country or community that observes Songkran; customs can also vary within a country.

Thailand

A national holiday from April 13 to 15. Water fights fill Bangkok's Silom Road and Khao San Road and the old city of Chiang Mai.

Lao communities

Pi Mai Lao is a related but distinct Lao New Year, celebrated with temple visits, processions, respectful water blessings and festivities, especially in Luang Prabang.

Myanmar communities

Thingyan is Myanmar's related but distinct New Year water festival, combining water play with merit-making, respect for elders and charitable acts.

Cambodian communities

Choul Chnam Thmey is Cambodia's related but distinct April New Year, observed with temple offerings, family rituals, games and some water customs.

Tai communities in China, India and neighboring countries

Dai and other Tai peoples hold closely related April water and New Year festivals, with local names, Buddhist observances and community rituals.

Sri Lanka & parts of South Asia

Sinhala and Tamil New Year and several solar New Years occur at a similar astrological transition, but they are distinct traditions and are not Songkran.

How long is Songkran?

The official public holiday runs from April 13 to April 15, and celebrations in some areas last longer.

Is Songkran celebrated outside Thailand?

Yes. Similar new-year water festivals are held in Laos (Pi Mai), Myanmar (Thingyan), Cambodia and parts of southern China.
