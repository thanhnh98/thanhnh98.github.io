About Halloween

Costumes, pumpkins and trick-or-treating on October 31.

Also known as: All Hallows' Eve, Hallowe'en

Halloween falls on October 31 every year, the eve of All Saints' Day. Its roots trace back to the Celtic festival of Samhain, when people lit bonfires and wore disguises at the turn of the season.

Today it is one of the biggest celebrations in the United States, Canada, Ireland and the UK, known for costume parties, carved jack-o'-lanterns, haunted houses and children going door to door for candy.

Origins & communities

Historical roots
Halloween developed from a blend of the Gaelic festival of Samhain and the Christian observance of All Hallows' Eve in Ireland and Britain.
Communities & traditions
It remains especially prominent in Ireland, the United Kingdom, the United States and Canada, and is now enjoyed in many countries as a largely secular community celebration.

Traditions

  • Trick-or-treating in costume
  • Carving jack-o'-lanterns
  • Costume parties and parades
  • Haunted houses and scary movies

Halloween Around the World

These examples cover major regional and diaspora traditions. They are representative, not an exhaustive list of every country or community that observes Halloween; customs can also vary within a country.

United States & Canada

Trick-or-treating, costume parties and carved pumpkins make Halloween one of the biggest celebrations of the year.

Ireland

Halloween grew out of the Celtic festival of Samhain. Bonfires and barmbrack fruit bread are still part of the night, and schools usually have a mid-term break around it.

United Kingdom

Costumes, parties and trick-or-treating are widespread, especially among children. Bonfire Night on November 5 is a separate British observance.

Australia & New Zealand

Costume parties and trick-or-treating have become increasingly visible, although Halloween is not a public holiday and participation varies by neighborhood.

Japan, South Korea & Southeast Asia

Cities and theme parks often host costume parades and commercial events; observance is mostly secular and concentrated in urban areas.

Mexico & Latin America

Halloween is observed in some communities, but Mexico's Día de Muertos and Catholic All Saints/All Souls traditions are distinct commemorations and should not be treated as the same holiday.

Is Halloween a public holiday?

No. Halloween is not a federal or public holiday in the United States or most other countries, so schools and offices usually stay open.

What time does trick-or-treating start?

Most US neighborhoods trick-or-treat in the early evening of October 31, typically from around 5 PM to 8 PM local time. Many towns publish official hours.
