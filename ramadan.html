About Ramadan

The holy month of fasting, from the crescent moon.

Also known as: Ramzan, Ramazan, Ramadhan, Ramadan Kareem

Ramadan is the ninth month of the Islamic calendar, observed by Muslims worldwide with fasting from dawn to sunset. Because the Islamic calendar is lunar, Ramadan moves about 11 days earlier each year.

The month begins when the new crescent moon is sighted and lasts 29 or 30 days. Days are shaped by suhoor before dawn, iftar at sunset and extra night prayers (Taraweeh), and the month ends with Eid al-Fitr.

Origins & communities

Historical roots
Ramadan is the ninth month of the Islamic calendar. Its religious significance is tied to the first revelations of the Quran to the Prophet Muhammad in the seventh century, and fasting became a central practice of Islam.
Communities & traditions
It is observed by Muslims worldwide—including Sunni, Shia and other Muslim communities—across the Middle East, Africa, Asia, Europe, the Americas and Oceania. Local moon-sighting and cultural traditions differ by place.

Traditions

  • Fasting from dawn to sunset
  • Suhoor and iftar meals
  • Taraweeh night prayers
  • Charity (zakat) and reading the Quran

Ramadan Around the World

These examples cover major regional and diaspora traditions. They are representative, not an exhaustive list of every country or community that observes Ramadan; customs can also vary within a country.

Saudi Arabia & the Gulf

Ramadan dates follow the Umm al-Qura calendar and moon sighting, and working hours are usually shortened.

Jordan & the Levant

Jordan confirms the start after the crescent moon is investigated. Family iftars, neighborhood charity, evening gatherings and qatayef sweets are widely associated with the month.

Egypt & North Africa

Communities combine fasting and nightly worship with family iftars, charity and distinctive local customs such as Ramadan lanterns in Egypt and late-night gatherings across the Maghreb.

Indonesia

Home to the world's largest Muslim population. The government announces the start of Ramadan after a sidang isbat (moon-sighting session).

Malaysia, Singapore & Brunei

Ramadan features communal prayers, bazaars and family iftars; official religious authorities announce the month's beginning locally.

Pakistan, India, Bangladesh & Sri Lanka

Ramadan, often called Ramzan, follows local religious announcements; bazaars, mosque prayers, charity and regional iftar foods shape the month.

Turkey, the Balkans & Central Asia

Ramazan is marked by fasting, communal iftars and night prayers. Turkey publishes dates by astronomical calculation, while customs vary across Muslim communities in the wider region.

West & East Africa

Large Muslim communities from Senegal and Nigeria to Sudan, Somalia, Kenya and Tanzania combine fasting and worship with shared meals, charity and local foods.

Europe, the Americas & Oceania

Muslim communities organize mosque prayers, community iftars and charity. Local moon sighting, overseas announcements or calculated calendars can produce different start dates.
