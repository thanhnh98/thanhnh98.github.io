About Ramadan

The holy month of fasting, from the crescent moon.

Also known as: Ramzan, Ramazan, Ramadhan, Ramadan Kareem

Ramadan is the ninth month of the Islamic calendar, observed by Muslims worldwide with fasting from dawn to sunset. Because the Islamic calendar is lunar, Ramadan moves about 11 days earlier each year.

The month begins when the new crescent moon is sighted and lasts 29 or 30 days. Days are shaped by suhoor before dawn, iftar at sunset and extra night prayers (Taraweeh), and the month ends with Eid al-Fitr.

Traditions

  • Fasting from dawn to sunset
  • Suhoor and iftar meals
  • Taraweeh night prayers
  • Charity (zakat) and reading the Quran

Ramadan Around the World

Saudi Arabia & the Gulf

Ramadan dates follow the Umm al-Qura calendar and moon sighting, and working hours are usually shortened.

Indonesia

Home to the world's largest Muslim population. The government announces the start of Ramadan after a sidang isbat (moon-sighting session).

Pakistan, India & Bangladesh

Ramadan, often called Ramzan, is based on local moon sighting and can begin a day after Saudi Arabia.

Turkey

Ramazan dates are set in advance by astronomical calculation.

United Kingdom, United States & Canada

Mosques and communities follow either local moon sighting or the Saudi announcement, so start dates can differ by a day.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many days until Ramadan?

There are 136 days until Ramadan 2027, which is expected on Monday, February 8, 2027. The live countdown on this page updates every second.

When is Ramadan 2028?

Why does Ramadan start on a different date every year?

The Islamic calendar has about 354 days, so Ramadan shifts roughly 11 days earlier in the Gregorian calendar each year.

Why might Ramadan start on a different day in my country?

Many communities begin Ramadan after a local sighting of the crescent moon, which can differ by a day from the Umm al-Qura calendar used here.
