About Chinese New Year

The Lunar New Year, also celebrated as Tết in Vietnam.

Also known as: Lunar New Year, Spring Festival, Tết, Seollal

Chinese New Year, also called the Spring Festival, is the first day of the lunar calendar. It falls on the second new moon after the winter solstice, between January 21 and February 20.

It is the most important holiday in China and is also celebrated across East and Southeast Asia, including as Tết in Vietnam and Seollal in Korea. Families reunite for a New Year's Eve dinner, give red envelopes and welcome the new zodiac animal.

Origins & communities

Historical roots
The Spring Festival grew from ancient Chinese agrarian and lunisolar calendar traditions marking renewal, ancestors and the beginning of spring.
Communities & traditions
Related Lunar New Year traditions are celebrated by Chinese communities and across East and Southeast Asia. Tết in Vietnam and Seollal in Korea are distinct national traditions with their own histories and customs.

Traditions

  • Reunion dinner on New Year's Eve
  • Red envelopes (hongbao) with lucky money
  • Lion and dragon dances
  • Fireworks and red decorations

Chinese New Year Around the World

These examples cover major regional and diaspora traditions. They are representative, not an exhaustive list of every country or community that observes Chinese New Year; customs can also vary within a country.

China

The Spring Festival holiday sparks the world's largest annual human migration as people travel home for the reunion dinner.

Taiwan, Hong Kong & Macao

Families gather for reunion meals, visit relatives, give red envelopes and attend temple or public festivities over a multi-day holiday.

Vietnam

Tết Nguyên Đán is Vietnam's largest traditional holiday, with ancestor remembrance, family reunions, bánh chưng or bánh tét, flowers and lucky money. It is a distinct Vietnamese tradition.

South Korea

Seollal is a distinct Korean New Year tradition centered on family reunions, sebae bows to elders, tteokguk and, in many households, ancestral rites called charye.

Singapore, Malaysia & Brunei

Chinese communities celebrate with reunion meals, red packets, visiting and lion dances; Chinese New Year is a public holiday in Singapore and Malaysia.

Indonesia, Thailand & the Philippines

Chinese and Peranakan communities hold temple visits, family meals, red-envelope giving and public cultural events; Indonesia recognizes Lunar New Year as a national holiday.

United States, Canada, United Kingdom, Australia & New Zealand

Chinese and wider Asian diaspora communities organize family celebrations, Chinatown parades, lion dances and lantern events.

Other Lunar New Year communities

Mongolian Tsagaan Sar, Tibetan Losar and other lunisolar New Years may fall near the same period, but they are distinct holidays with their own calendars and traditions.
