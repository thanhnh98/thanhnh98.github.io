About Eid al-Fitr

The Festival of Breaking the Fast at the end of Ramadan.

Also known as: Eid ul-Fitr, Eid, Hari Raya Aidilfitri, Lebaran, Ramazan Bayramı

Eid al-Fitr is the celebration that marks the end of Ramadan. It falls on the first day of Shawwal, the tenth month of the Islamic calendar, once the new crescent moon has been sighted.

The day begins with a special Eid prayer, followed by visits to family and friends, festive meals and gifts for children. Before the prayer, Muslims give Zakat al-Fitr to those in need.

Origins & communities

Historical roots
Eid al-Fitr began in the first Muslim community in Medina in the seventh century as the feast marking the completion of Ramadan.
Communities & traditions
It is celebrated by Muslims worldwide—including Sunni, Shia and other Muslim communities—with shared religious practices and diverse regional foods, clothing and family customs.

Traditions

  • Eid prayer in the morning
  • Zakat al-Fitr charity
  • New clothes and family visits
  • Sweets and festive meals

Eid al-Fitr Around the World

These examples cover major regional and diaspora traditions. They are representative, not an exhaustive list of every country or community that observes Eid al-Fitr; customs can also vary within a country.

Saudi Arabia & UAE

Eid al-Fitr is a public holiday lasting several days, with morning prayers, family visits and festive meals.

Jordan & the Levant

The day begins with Eid prayer, followed by family visits, gifts or eidiya for children, and sweets such as ma'amoul and date-filled ka'ak.

Egypt & North Africa

Families attend Eid prayer, visit relatives and share regional sweets; in Egypt, kahk biscuits and outings are especially associated with the feast.

Indonesia

Known as Lebaran. Millions of people travel home for mudik, the annual homecoming.

Malaysia & Singapore

Hari Raya Aidilfitri is a public holiday, and families host open houses for friends and neighbors.

Pakistan, India, Bangladesh & Sri Lanka

Eid follows Chand Raat and local moon announcements, with morning prayer, new clothes, family visits, henna, gifts and sweets such as sheer khurma.

Turkey, the Balkans & Central Asia

Ramazan Bayramı or related local names mark a multi-day period of prayer, visiting elders, hospitality and sweets.

West & East Africa

From Senegal and Nigeria to Sudan, Somalia and coastal East Africa, communities gather for prayer, dress in festive clothing and share meals, gifts and charity.

Europe, the Americas & Oceania

Muslim communities gather in mosques, parks and community halls for prayer and festivals, maintaining family traditions while adapting to local school and work calendars.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many days until Eid al-Fitr?

When is Eid al-Fitr 2028?

How long is Eid al-Fitr?

Religiously it is one day, but many countries give a public holiday of three days or more.

What is the difference between Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha?

Eid al-Fitr ends Ramadan. Eid al-Adha, about 70 days later, marks the Hajj pilgrimage and the sacrifice of Ibrahim.
