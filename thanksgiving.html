About Thanksgiving

The fourth Thursday of November in the United States.

Also known as: Thanksgiving Day, Turkey Day

Thanksgiving in the United States is a federal holiday held on the fourth Thursday of November. It has been fixed to that Thursday by law since 1941.

It is traditionally a day for family gatherings and a large meal centered on roast turkey. The long weekend also marks the start of the holiday shopping season, with Black Friday the following day.

Origins & communities

Historical roots
Thanksgiving grew from European harvest observances and colonial days of thanksgiving. The modern US holiday draws on the 1621 Plymouth harvest story, whose legacy is viewed differently by many Native peoples.
Communities & traditions
The United States, Canada and Liberia observe distinct Thanksgiving holidays, while harvest-thanksgiving traditions also exist in parts of Europe and elsewhere.

Traditions

Thanksgiving Around the World

These examples cover major regional and diaspora traditions. They are representative, not an exhaustive list of every country or community that observes Thanksgiving; customs can also vary within a country.

United States

A federal holiday on the fourth Thursday of November. The next day is Black Friday, the traditional start of holiday shopping.

Canada

Canadian Thanksgiving is earlier, on the second Monday of October, and is a statutory holiday in most provinces.

Liberia

Liberia marks its own Thanksgiving Day on the first Thursday of November.

Grenada

October 25 is a national Thanksgiving Day commemorating the events of 1983; its history and meaning differ from US Thanksgiving.

Norfolk Island

The Australian territory keeps a local Thanksgiving tradition shaped by American whalers and island church communities.

Germany and other European communities

Erntedankfest and other harvest thanksgiving services express gratitude for the harvest, but they are distinct observances rather than the US holiday counted on this page.
